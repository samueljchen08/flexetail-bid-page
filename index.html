How the number is built

Method

1. Takeoff. The project is reduced to quantities: containers by use, square feet of envelope and floor, linear feet of guardrail, square feet of LED, loads times miles, and so on. Those quantities come from a spec, a SketchUp export, or a reading of the renderings.

2. Unit prices. Each quantity is priced from a cost book of assemblies. Every assembly carries a low, mid, and high installed unit price. Low is a favorable quote, a volume buy, or a simple condition. Mid is typical 2025 Southeast US installed cost. High is a premium spec or a difficult condition. Each line's sources, the figures they quote, and the rationale for the range are listed below.

3. Factors. Factory lines are scaled by a North Carolina cost index and field lines by the site's index (Atlanta); interior and opening lines are scaled by finish and glazing level.

4. Markups. General conditions, permits, and contingency are percentages of hard cost; overhead and profit is a percentage of hard cost plus general conditions. Every lever flows through to these.

5. Risk. The planning range is a 3,000-run Monte Carlo: each line is drawn from a triangular distribution across its low / mid / high, and one shared 0.80 / 1.00 / 1.50 factor per run represents the correlated risk a concept-stage estimate carries (scope growth, escalation, site unknowns). That factor is the AACE Class 5 accuracy band; the sources section cites it.

What it is not. A rough order of magnitude, not a bid. The honest next step is replacing the mid of each big line with a real quote; the sensitivity chart says which lines to quote first.
